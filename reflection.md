# Reflection — Assignment Reminder

### What I Got Wrong at the Start
When I started this project, I assumed students simply needed another reminder list or calendar alarm app where they could type in their deadlines. But when I went to Hostel K and sat down with Ankit, he immediately pointed out the real friction: students already get notifications from Google Classroom, but they swipe them away and forget hours later. More importantly, he said nobody is going to manually open an app and type assignment titles and due dates when the professor has already posted everything online. That conversation completely overturned my initial plan. I realized a manual to-do tool was useless, and the only solution that would actually help Ankit was one that connects directly to Google Classroom API, pulls upcoming coursework automatically, and calculates the reminder window without any manual input.

### What I Would Cut
If I had to rebuild this within an even tighter timeframe, I would cut out all ambitions of building a multi-threshold configuration system or tracking whether an assignment was turned in. Initially, I thought about adding submission status verification and student grade tracking, but that overcomplicates what should be a dead-simple utility. A student in the hostel staring at an 11:59 PM deadline only needs one thing: an unmissable alert at 9:00 PM (3 hours before) telling them to open Classroom and start submitting. Keeping the tool focused strictly on that single 3-hour alert makes the database lightweight and removes unnecessary friction.

### Where AI Helped
AI was genuinely helpful for rapidly generating backend boilerplate that would have otherwise eaten up most of my 24 hours. Specifically, it helped structure the Google OAuth 2.0 integration with Passport.js, scaffold the `node-cron` background scheduler, and write the atomic MongoDB locking query (`findOneAndUpdate` transitioning from `SCHEDULED` to `SENDING`). Without that atomic transition, avoiding duplicate emails when the cron ticks every minute would have required complex external locking mechanisms.

### Where AI Wasted Time
AI caused significant delays when dealing with Google Cloud Console's recent dashboard overhaul. The suggestions repeatedly referred to the legacy "OAuth consent screen" layout, whereas Google Cloud now routes credentials through the new "Google Auth Platform" with separated "Audience" and "Branding" tabs. This led to confusing 403 `access_denied` and 404 `v3/warning` errors during login testing. Furthermore, AI failed to anticipate that privacy-focused browsers like Brave actively strip Google's redirect tokens and telemetry scripts, which caused Google's consent screen to break midway. Diagnosing that I needed to whitelist test users in the Google Cloud Console and turn Brave Shields down was something I had to figure out by inspecting network tabs myself rather than relying on AI suggestions.
